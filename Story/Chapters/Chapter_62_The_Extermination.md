# Chapter 62: The Extermination

The problem with embedded surveillance is that it only works if the target does not notice the bug in the system.

Victor stood on the observation deck with a magnifying glass in one hand and a ledger in the other, staring at a single golden mosquito perched on a mana-heavy vine. It pulsed faintly, as if someone had trapped a coin in insect form and taught it to breathe.

Beautiful. In a parasitic, lawsuit-ready sort of way.

"Valerius," Victor said without taking his eyes off the mosquito. "Current loss estimate."

The accountant flipped pages with the shaken precision of a man reading his own death certificate. "Each mosquito drains approximately 0.03% of ambient mana per hour. At the confirmed population of eighty-seven, that is roughly 12.4 GP per day."

"Compounding?"

Valerius swallowed. "Yes, Boss."

Victor did the math in his head.

Twelve gold a day sounded trivial to idiots. Trivial losses were how empires bled out. Compounding did not care whether a leak looked dramatic. It only cared that it existed.

Over a year, that was more than four thousand gold siphoned out of his infrastructure by a Bank-issued parasite with wings.

That was not an annoyance.

That was theft with branding.

"And they are linked to the Bank?"

"Confirmed." Valerius held up a loan appendix. "The mosquitoes are legally classified as Financial Monitoring Sprites. Section 47-C. Fine print says the lender reserves the right to deploy magical oversight mechanisms to preserve the asset."

Victor finally lowered the magnifying glass. "Did Malcom really think I would not read the fine print?"

"Most borrowers do not, Boss."

"Most borrowers are not me."

Below them, Sir Roland stood at the entrance to Floor 4 with his arms crossed, staring at the mosquitoes like they had personally insulted his ancestors. He had been doing that for ten minutes.

Victor leaned over the railing. "Officer! Question."

Roland looked up. "Heretic."

"Are those unlicensed magical constructs?"

A pause.

Roland opened his compliance manual, a massive tome bound in steel and sanctified leather. He turned a page, then another, lips moving as he read.

"Definition: magical construct," he said at last. "Any entity created, summoned, or deployed by spell, ritual, or enchantment that operates autonomously within Crown-regulated territory."

He looked up again.

"Yes. Those are Class-C magical vermin. Unlicensed."

Victor smiled.

There it was. The crack in the wall.

"And the penalty?"

Roland flipped to the relevant section. "Immediate containment. Submission of Pest Control Form 38-B within seventy-two hours. Failure to comply results in a five hundred GP fine and suspension of operational permits."

"Excellent," Victor said. "I’ll file the paperwork."

Roland’s expression hardened. "You are planning to kill them."

"No. I am planning to relocate them."

***

**The Capture Operation**

Sniv held the butterfly net with both hands and entirely too much enthusiasm. The net was enchanted silk woven through with copper thread. Fifteen GP from the Market Quarter. Cheap, reliable, and just legal enough to be boring.

"Boss want Sniv to catch bugs?"

"Correct," Victor said. "Non-lethally. We need one intact specimen for analysis."

Gorg crouched nearby, massive hands cupped together as if he expected the mosquitoes to personally surrender. "Gorg can clap?"

"No clapping," Victor said immediately. "That triggers the self-destruct clause."

Gorg blinked. "They have self-destruct?"

Valerius lifted the loan contract and pointed to a paragraph written in the sort of font size usually reserved for threats and poison labels. "If the monitoring sprites are tampered with, the Bank can call the loan in early. All ten thousand GP. Due immediately."

Gorg made a low sound of disgust. "Bank sneaky."

"Bank competent," Victor corrected. "Which makes them dangerous."

He watched Sniv creep toward the vine.

One mosquito hovered near a cluster of glowing orchids, its proboscis buried in the mana-rich flower like a bureaucrat with a private tax exemption.

Sniv swung.

The net dropped cleanly.

The insect buzzed wildly, but did not explode.

"Got one!" Sniv cheered.

"Good. Bring it to the workshop."

***

**The Dissection**

Valerius laid the captured mosquito on a stone table surrounded by detection runes and anti-magic dampeners. The insect’s wings vibrated weakly against the net.

"Alright," Valerius muttered, drawing on his gloves. "Let’s see what kind of monster the Bank calls a service feature."

He cast **[Arcane Autopsy]**.

The mosquito’s magical structure lit up in the air above the table like a corporate org chart from hell.

Three cores.

1. **Mana Extraction Node** — proboscis. Siphoned 0.03% mana per hour.
2. **Data Transmission Core** — thorax. Reported location, profitability, and operational stability.
3. **Self-Destruct Trigger** — abdomen. Detonated if removed, killed, or magically suppressed.

Valerius stared. "It is a tripwire. Kill one and Malcom gets an alert. Kill all of them and he triggers the repayment clause."

Victor nodded slowly.

So that was the shape of the problem.

Not a swarm. A contract.

"We’re trapped?" Valerius whispered.

"No," Victor said. "We’re incentivized. There’s a difference."

He pulled up ARMI.

> **[ARMI]**
> **Threat Assessment**: Financial Parasites
> **Current Loss Rate**: 12.4 GP/day
> **Projected Annual Loss**: 4,526 GP
> **Contract Breach Risk**: 94% if sprites terminated
> **Status**: CRITICAL — COMPLIANCE SOLUTION REQUIRED

Victor stared at the schematic for a full thirty seconds.

Then sixty.

The room stayed quiet except for the faint buzz of the mosquito in the net.

Three options emerged.

Option A: kill the mosquitoes and trigger the repayment clause.

ROI: catastrophically negative.

Option B: ignore them and let them bleed him forever.

ROI: still negative, just slower.

Option C: exploit the wording.

That one had teeth.

Victor smiled.

"Valerius. Get Roland. And draft a formal complaint."

The accountant blinked. "A complaint? Against the Bank?"

"Against unlicensed magical constructs operating on my property."

Valerius looked like he wanted to lie down on the floor and wait for the universe to end.

"Boss, that is the Bank."

"No," Victor said. "That is leverage."

***

**The Bureaucratic Gambit**

Thirty minutes later, Sir Roland stood in the workshop reading Victor’s complaint as if it had personally offended him.

**FORMAL COMPLAINT — MAGICAL PEST INFESTATION**

**Filed by**: Victor Kaine, Administrator of Dungeon Insolvia

**Violation**: Unlicensed deployment of Class-C magical constructs

**Statute**: Magical Pest Control Act of 1447, Section 12

**Relief Requested**:
- Authorization to relocate invasive magical species to designated containment zone
- Waiver of liability for incidental disruption to third-party monitoring systems
- Seventy-two-hour compliance window per standard procedure

Roland read it once.

Then again.

Then he lowered the paper and looked at Victor.

"You are not killing them," he said carefully.

"Correct. I am relocating them to a jar."

Roland stared at the glass container on the table.

A jar.

A very enchanted jar.

Inside, one mosquito buzzed angrily against the preservation runes.

"A jar does not count as extraction, " Victor said. "Technically, the Bank still has oversight access. They just lose the ability to drain mana. The loan terms never specify where the monitoring device must be located."

Roland’s jaw tightened. "This is absurd."

"It is legal."

Roland looked from the complaint to the jar to the insect and back again.

His expression suggested the pain of a man discovering that reality was governed by people like Victor.

At last, he stamped the document.

**APPROVED.**

"You have seventy-two hours," Roland said. "If the Bank objects, this will go to Crown Court."

Victor took the paper. "Let them object."

***

**The Counter-Offer**

Victor did not wait for the Bank to react.

He drafted a letter immediately. Formal tone. Clean language. No insult, no flourish, no wasted emotion. Which made it more threatening than a shouted curse.

He sent it by registered magical pigeon.

---

**TO**: Director Malcom, Senior Loans Officer

**FROM**: Victor Kaine, Administrator

**RE**: Pest Relocation Notice

Director Malcom,

Crown Compliance Officer Sir Roland has identified the Financial Monitoring Sprites deployed on my property as unlicensed magical constructs in violation of the Magical Pest Control Act of 1447.

Per Crown law, I am required to relocate these entities to a designated containment zone within seventy-two hours.

I have enclosed a proposal:

1. Reduce sprite population by 80% via voluntary recall.
2. Remaining sprites may continue monitoring only — extraction capability disabled.
3. In exchange, I will not pursue legal action for unlicensed surveillance.
4. Additional consideration: waive the first quarter of compound interest, amounting to 500 GP.

Failure to respond within 24 hours will result in full relocation and a formal complaint filed with the Banking Regulatory Commission.

Regards,
Victor Kaine

---

Victor sealed the letter and tied it to the pigeon’s leg.

"Fly fast," he told the bird.

It shot out of the window like it had bills to pay.

Valerius stared at him in horrified admiration. "Boss... you just threatened the Bank."

"No," Victor said. "I gave them an exit strategy."

***

**The Response**

Twelve hours later, a second pigeon returned.

The paper it carried was short.

Very short.

---

**FROM**: Director Malcom

**TO**: Mr. Kaine

Your complaint has been reviewed.

The Financial Monitoring Sprites were installed per standard operating procedure. However, we acknowledge certain territorial compliance issues.

Proposal accepted:
- 80% sprite recall effective immediately
- Remaining sprites: monitoring only, no extraction
- First quarter compound interest waived

Consider this matter closed.

We look forward to continued partnership.

— M.

---

Victor read the letter twice.

Then he laughed.

It was short, sharp, and honest.

"He blinked."

Valerius looked shocked enough to forget how to breathe. "The Bank actually negotiated."

"Of course he did," Victor said. "If this went to Crown Court, the precedent would be poison. Every dungeon, every merchant, every noble with a loan would start auditing for illegal surveillance. The Church would use it to justify inspections on every financial institution in the kingdom."

He folded the note once and set it aside.

"Malcom cut his losses. Competent decision."

By sunset, the mosquito population had been reduced to fifteen. The remaining sprites hovered passively, their extraction nodes visibly deactivated, their little golden bodies now only surveillance devices instead of theft engines.

Roland inspected each one personally.

"They are compliant," he said at last, sounding annoyed that the universe had failed to produce a clean conviction.

"Excellent," Victor said. "File the closure report."

***

**The Farm**

That night, Victor sat in his office inventorying the captured mosquitoes.

Seventy-two jars lined the table, each preserving a golden insect in perfect suspended irritation.

"What do we do with them, Boss?" Valerius asked.

Victor picked up one jar and held it up to the light.

The mosquito inside shimmered.

Inside its abdomen, beneath the self-destruct core, something glowed.

A tiny crystallized mana fragment.

Victor stared.

> **[ARMI]**
> **Item**: Golden Mosquito Core
> **Type**: Mana Battery / Regulator Component
> **Estimated Market Value**: 5 GP

He did the math.

Seventy-two mosquitoes.

Five GP each.

Three hundred and sixty GP sitting on his table because the Bank had been arrogant enough to weaponize breeding stock.

"They were not just surveillance," Victor said softly. "They were loot."

Valerius blinked. "The Bank gave us a renewable resource."

"Accidentally, yes."

Victor set the jar down.

And then the better idea arrived.

Not through inspiration.

Through greed.

"Start a mosquito farm. Breeding program. If the Bank wants to plant financial parasites in my infrastructure, I’m going to cultivate them."

Sniv’s eyes lit up so hard it looked like someone had struck flint.

"Sniv farm bugs! Sniv good at farming!"

"Excellent," Victor said. "Design the cages. Feed them mana-rich runoff. I want breeding pairs, yield tracking, and a sanitation plan. If they can grow on my property, they can pay rent."

Sniv saluted with the butterfly net.

"Sniv make bug empire."

"That is the spirit."

As Sniv scurried off muttering about terrariums and mana fertilizer, a new pigeon landed on the windowsill.

Victor unfolded the message.

One sentence.

> *Clever. But you just made it personal.*
> — M.

Victor crumpled the note.

Then he smiled.

"Good," he said. "Personal is profitable."

Down in the jungle, the fifteen remaining monitoring sprites buzzed silently among the vines.

And in a vault beneath the city, Director Malcom closed one ledger and opened another.

The game had changed.

He just did not seem to understand who had set the board.

---

**ARMI**
> **Bank Compliance Status**: PARTIAL RECALL EXECUTED
> **New Asset Class**: BIOLOGICAL MONITORING STOCK
> **Projected Revenue**: 360 GP immediate / 1,800 GP annual potential
> **Hostility Index**: INCREASING
> **Status**: BANK HAS DECLARED PERSONAL WAR

Victor leaned back in his chair and looked at the little golden prison on his desk.

A bug.

A ledger entry.

A reason to expand.

"Open the next floor plans," he said.

Outside, far beyond the dungeon walls, the Bank began to move.

And for the first time since Victor had landed in this world, the parasites were not hiding.

They were coming for him openly.
